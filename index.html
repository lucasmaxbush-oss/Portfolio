Sheet 01 Milwaukee Tool · Packaging co-op · Jun to Dec 2025

Inserts that fly marketing samples without damage

Role: design, prototyping, ISTA test reporting

Marketing samples travel constantly, to shoots, to trade shows, to distributors, and they travel by air. Rough handling, no second chance if a tool arrives scuffed. My job was to design custom inserts that fit inside the Packout tool cases the company already sells, so a sample kit protects itself and arrives looking deliberate.

The three kits use two different processes. The large bit set is cut foam, which suits the wide, shallow pockets that set needs. The drill kit and the OMT kit are 3D printed in TPU instead. Foam was tearing at the thin sections those layouts demanded, and TPU holds its shape there while delivering more cushioning over a shorter deformation range, which is what the smaller cases have room for.

I ran ISTA testing across a lot of products during the co-op and wrote and presented those reports, but the feedback that actually shaped these inserts came from somewhere else: people seeing the marketing samples in the field, then asking whether they could get one.

Sheet 02 Senior capstone · PKSC 4200 · Spring 2025

Capstone: two ways to pack a mug

Role: structural design and technical drawing

The design requirements were a fiber based packaging solution to ship custom printed mugs straight to consumer. Two mug sizes had to be packaged with the same dieline. The restrictions imposed required the weight of the mug and packaging together to be less than 1 pound, and the folding process to take less than one minute.

That called for two answers. A production shipper that could run at 10,000 units a month on a die cut, glued B flute blank, and a premium "Cadillac" design with no cost ceiling, judged on the unboxing experience but still required to pass ISTA 3A.

The “Cadillac” Telescoping box · coated E flute · passed ISTA 3A

The premium style packaging had to be memorable while still passing ISTA 3A. That was achieved using a three part telescoping box in mottled white coated E flute, chosen for the shape it holds and the print quality it carries.

In order to pass ISTA testing, an outer cherry lock box was used to hold foam above and below the mug. Both the 11 oz and 15 oz mugs survived testing.

Maximum drop height The survival challenge

The final component of the project was to design a package that would protect the mug from the greatest possible height. By using the largest box producible in the lab for maximum air resistance, and stacked cones to act as a crumple zone, the box was able to survive drop testing and could potentially survive impact at its terminal velocity.

Sheet 03 Clemson flexographic printing lab

Organization in a working print lab

Role: press support, lab organization

The cleaning cart was in need of organization. Rags and bottles were left loose with nowhere to go during time sensitive cleaning. The cleaning cart insert uses the existing geometry of the cart to secure itself. The bottle insert has enough capacity to provide cleaning solutions for two lab assistants at once, as well as separate the reusable rags from the lint free cloth.

The sponge hanger also needed a redesign, to increase its storage capacity and secure it to the cart in a way that eliminates rocking while still allowing it to be removable.

Sheet 04 Corrugated structures · Clemson

An ergonomic chair made of corrugate

Role: internal support design, load testing, prototyping

The chair structure is a stack of corrugate sheets with the corrugated fluting oriented vertically. The outer structure of the chair has dimensions based on an MIT ergonomics study. The chair was tested before prototyping was complete, to avoid producing bad product.

The internals of the chair consist of six threaded rods, twelve one inch washers, and twelve bolts tightened until secure.
