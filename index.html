Sheet 01 Milwaukee Tool · Packaging co-op · Jun to Dec 2025

Packout inserts for marketing samples

Role: design, prototyping, ISTA test reporting

The marketing department used to transport loose samples in a contractor bag. It was slow to work out of, hazardous to reach into, and hard on the samples in air travel. I was tasked with designing something better from scratch.

The three Packout kits used two different manufacturing processes. The large bit set is made from multiple layers of foam topped with a sheet of plastic corrugate, which lets the sharp bits sit tightly in their cells and still be removed easily. The drill and OMT kits were produced using 3D printed TPU. That was necessary because of the thin cushioning space between the tools and the case: TPU holds its shape well while still providing some cushioning.

The clearest signal these worked came from customers who saw the samples in person and asked when they would be available for purchase.

Sheet 02 Senior capstone · PKSC 4200 · Spring 2025

Capstone: three ways to pack a mug

Role: structural design, ideation, technical drawing, and prototyping

There were three parts of the capstone project. A production shipper, a “Cadillac” design and a maximum drop height challenge.

The design requirements for a production shipper were a fiber based packaging solution to ship custom printed mugs direct to consumer. Two mugs of different sizes had to be packaged with the same deadline. The restrictions imposed required the weight of the mug and package together to be less than 1 pound, the folding process to take less than one minute, and the corrugate usage to be less than the sponsor's original design.

The premium Cadillac design had no cost ceiling. It was judged based on unboxing experience while still passing ISTA 3A.

The maximum drop height challenge was a fun way to close off the semester with some friendly competition.

The “Cadillac” Telescoping box · coated E flute · passed ISTA 3A

The premium style packaging had to be memorable while still passing ISTA 3A. That was achieved using a three part telescoping box in mottled white coated E flute, chosen for the shape it holds and the print quality it carries.

In order to pass ISTA testing, an outer cherry lock box was used to hold foam above and below the mug. Both the 11 oz and 15 oz mugs survived testing.

Sheet 03 Clemson flexographic printing lab

Organization in a working print lab

Role: press support, lab organization

The cleaning cart was in need of organization. Rags and bottles were left loose, without a designated spot to go during time-sensitive cleaning. The cleaning cart insert uses the existing geometry of the cart to secure itself. The bottle insert has enough capacity to hold cleaning solutions for two lab assistants at once, and it separates the reusable rags from the lint-free cloths.

The sponge hanger also needed a redesign, to increase its storage capacity and secure it to the cart in a way that eliminated rocking while allowing it to be removed.

Sheet 04 Corrugated structures · Clemson

An ergonomic chair made of corrugate

Role: internal support design, load testing, prototyping

The chair structure is a stack of corrugate sheets with the corrugated fluting oriented vertically. The outer structure of the chair has dimensions based on an MIT ergonomics study. The chair was tested before prototyping was complete, to avoid producing bad product.

The internals of the chair consist of six threaded rods, twelve one inch washers, and twelve bolts tightened until secure. The bolts, washers and nuts are covered by corrugated plugs, which are removable so the stack can be tightened again in the future.
